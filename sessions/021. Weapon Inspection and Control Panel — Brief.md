## Phases

### Phase 1 — Decide the two open design questions

- How a weapon gets "selected" on the battlefield to bring up its info card — a click/tap on
  the weapon's own board cell, a new selection mode toggled independently of the existing
  place/target tools, or something reusing an interaction that already exists (`Cursor.luau`'s
  crosshair, `Input.getBoardCellUnderMouse`).
- How a disabled weapon (one with `structure.autoFire`/an equivalent "off" state, once this
  entry wires a real control to it) reads visually on the battlefield, distinct from a normal
  live weapon and from one still under construction.

Settle both before touching implementation code — see "Open questions" below for the specific
tradeoffs worth weighing.

### Phase 2 — Build the info card and the control

Per the plan entry's success criterion: selecting a placed weapon surfaces a bottom-right info
card with its live stats (range, damage, cooldown, current target) and a control to enable/
disable its automatic firing without bulldozing and replacing it. Session 015's finding means
the underlying flag already exists (`structure.autoFire`) — this phase is UI plus the command
plumbing to flip it, not new rules-core state. Leave room in the card's layout for upgrade
controls later, per the success criterion, without building anything upgrade-specific now.

## Open questions

Settle these at or before the Open Gate:

- **Selection interaction:** a new persistent "select" mode (like today's place/target tools)
  that a player toggles into, or a lighter-weight click-anywhere-on-a-live-weapon that doesn't
  compete with the existing tool state machine? The BuildPalette's tool model is one clear
  precedent to extend or deliberately diverge from.
- **Disabled-weapon visual:** a distinct color/material tint on the structure itself (mirroring
  how `Cursor.luau`'s green/red preview already signals state), a small icon/billboard, or
  something else entirely — needs to read clearly at a glance during live play, not just in the
  info card.
